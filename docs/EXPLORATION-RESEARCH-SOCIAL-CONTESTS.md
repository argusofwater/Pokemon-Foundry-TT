# Exploration, Research, Social Play, and Contests

## Core principle

All noncombat systems use the same core check:

`1d20 + Rank + situational modifier`

No separate resolution engine is required.

## Exploration structure

Exploration uses two scales:

- Scene: a short location-focused challenge with one objective, obstacle, consequence, and opportunity for Pokémon capabilities
- Expedition: a longer journey that tracks Progress, time, supplies, weather, fatigue, hazards, discoveries, and encounter risk

## Expedition roles

At the start of an Expedition, each trainer may choose one role and may change roles after a Camp Rest.

- Guide: route choice, navigation, safe terrain, and travel speed
- Scout: hazard detection, ambush detection, pathfinding, and locating wild Pokémon
- Quartermaster: supplies, equipment, camp, and consumption control
- Medic: Wounds, conditions, Fatigue, and rest quality
- Researcher: data collection, species study, and Research Progress
- Handler: Pokémon morale, Bond, calming, and disobedience prevention

A Pokémon may assist when it has a relevant capability, Talent, move, or sense.

## Expedition Progress

Suggested Progress targets:

- Short route: 3
- Regional journey: 6
- Difficult expedition: 9
- Major wilderness arc: 12+

Each travel interval, usually half a day, the Guide makes the Expedition check.

- Success: gain 1 Progress
- Strong success, beating the target by 5 or more: gain 2 Progress or a useful side benefit
- Failure: gain no Progress and suffer one complication
- Severe failure, missing by 5 or more: suffer a major complication such as lost time, extra supply use, Fatigue, hazard, encounter, or becoming lost

## Group contribution

Each role may contribute once per interval.

A successful support check may:

- Grant +2 to the Expedition check
- Grant Favored on one hazard check
- Reduce supply use
- Reveal an opportunity
- Cancel one minor complication

Only two support bonuses may directly modify the main roll. Additional successes create side benefits.

## Supplies

Use Supply Units instead of tracking individual meals and water containers.

One Supply Unit supports one trainer and their active travel Pokémon for one day. Large or unusually demanding Pokémon may require more.

Supply consumption may be reduced through foraging, equipment, Talents, Pokémon capabilities, settlements, or vehicles. Harsh weather, injury, large Pokémon, and forced marches may increase consumption.

A creature without sufficient supplies gains Fatigue after the next travel interval.

## Fatigue

Use four Fatigue states:

- Fresh
- Tired
- Exhausted
- Spent

### Tired

- Hindered on Endurance checks
- Travel movement reduced by 1

### Exhausted

- Hindered on Physical and Focus checks
- Cannot fully benefit from a Breather
- Travel movement reduced by 2

### Spent

- Cannot continue strenuous travel safely
- Continuing risks a Wound

Fatigue recovers through Camp Rest, Full Rest, food, shelter, medicine, Talents, and suitable abilities.

## Hazards

Every hazard lists:

- Detection
- Avoidance
- Consequence
- Mitigation
- Relevant capabilities

Examples include collapsing ledges, flash floods, toxic spores, unstable machinery, extreme weather, and environmental Zones.

Hazards should be stored as draggable compendium entries.

## Pokémon capabilities

Capabilities may automatically solve simple obstacles, grant Favored, replace a trainer skill, open a new route, reduce travel time, or prevent consequences.

Examples:

- Fly
- Swim
- Burrow
- Climb
- Mount
- Glow
- Scent
- Tremorsense
- Telepathy
- Telekinesis
- Strength
- Small Frame
- Intangibility
- Heat
- Cold
- Electricity
- Plant Growth

Do not require a roll when a capability clearly solves the obstacle.

## Discoveries

Exploration may produce Discoveries such as:

- Species
- Habitat
- Resource
- Ruin
- Phenomenon
- Route
- Settlement
- Faction
- Artifact
- Environmental anomaly

Discoveries may grant Research Progress, map updates, capture opportunities, materials, tutor access, reputation, evolution catalysts, or story hooks.

Foundry should maintain a regional Discovery Journal or map.

## Research

Each research subject has a Research Rating:

- Simple: 3 Progress
- Unusual: 5 Progress
- Rare: 8 Progress
- Exceptional: 12 Progress
- Legendary: story-gated

Research subjects may include species, moves, abilities, habitats, artifacts, diseases, weather phenomena, evolution methods, fossils, and Legendary events.

Research Progress may be earned through observation, scanning, samples, interviews, records, experiments, care, battle data, and habitat study.

Research checks use the normal core check. Success grants 1 Progress. Strong success grants 2 Progress or a Discovery. Failure usually costs time, materials, or creates incomplete data rather than erasing prior progress.

## Research rewards

Completing research may unlock:

- Full species data
- Ability information
- Capture bonuses
- TM compatibility
- Evolution methods
- Tutor techniques
- Crafting recipes
- Environmental counters
- Reputation
- Talent access
- Story clues

## Pokédex knowledge tiers

Species information unlocks through four tiers:

- Unknown
- Observed
- Studied
- Mastered

Foundry should reveal fields automatically according to knowledge tier.

## Social encounters

Important NPCs use:

- Disposition
- Goal
- Leverage
- Patience

### Disposition

- Hostile
- Unfriendly
- Neutral
- Friendly
- Supportive

### Leverage

Leverage may be evidence, a favor, shared enemy, payment, emotional bond, reputation, research data, authority, promise, or threat.

Strong Leverage may reduce difficulty, grant Favored, make a request possible, change Disposition, or remove the need for a roll.

## Social approaches

Players choose an approach before rolling, such as:

- Persuade
- Bargain
- Deceive
- Intimidate
- Inspire
- Appeal to duty
- Present evidence
- Offer help
- Perform

The GM selects the relevant skill from the existing skill list.

## Social Progress and Patience

Complex social scenes use a small Progress track:

- Simple request: 1 Progress
- Meaningful negotiation: 3 Progress
- Major political or emotional scene: 5 Progress

Success gains 1 Progress. Strong success may gain 2. Failure creates a complication or reduces Patience.

Important NPCs usually have 2–4 Patience. At 0 Patience, the negotiation ends, escalates, or becomes harder.

## Pokémon in social play

Pokémon may affect social scenes through Bond, reputation, symbolism, empathy, telepathy, performance, intimidation, comfort, and emotional sensing. Their presence may help or harm depending on context.

## Reputation

Use separate Reputation tracks for important factions or communities.

Suggested ladder:

- Hated
- Distrusted
- Unknown
- Respected
- Honored
- Celebrated

Reputation affects prices, availability, licenses, access, information, favors, NPC Disposition, contest invitations, and tutor access.

## Contests

A Contest has:

- Category
- Theme
- Audience
- Judges
- Three rounds
- Momentum
- Appeal

Suggested categories:

- Beauty
- Cool
- Clever
- Cute
- Tough
- Freestyle

## Contest rounds

### Round 1: Entrance

Establish presence and theme through Performance, Influence, Bond, costume, equipment, or an entrance move.

### Round 2: Showcase

Demonstrate coordination, moves, creativity, and setup.

### Round 3: Finale

Use a signature performance, combo, or risk that rewards prior setup.

## Appeal

Each round uses the normal core check plus move, Talent, theme, and category modifiers.

Judges award:

- 0 Appeal for failure
- 1 Appeal for success
- 2 Appeal for strong success
- Bonus Appeal for originality, theme, or combo use

Highest Appeal wins. Ties use audience response, Finale score, penalties, judge preference, or sudden-death performance.

## Contest Momentum

Gain 1 Momentum when:

- A move matches the category
- Trainer and Pokémon coordinate
- A move continues a combo
- The audience reacts strongly
- A rival performance is successfully answered

Spend Momentum to:

- Add +2
- Reroll
- Extend a combo
- Resist disruption
- Improve Finale Appeal
- Trigger Performer Talents

Maximum Momentum is 3.

## Contest tags

Moves may have contest tags separate from combat typing:

- Flashy
- Graceful
- Powerful
- Clever
- Cute
- Dramatic
- Rhythmic
- Risky
- Combo Starter
- Combo Finisher
- Audience

## Contest interference

Some formats allow limited interference such as distraction, attention theft, combo disruption, Weather changes, or stage Terrain changes.

Interference should normally affect Appeal rather than HP and may use short-lived conditions such as Distracted, Off-Balance, Upstaged, or Flustered.
